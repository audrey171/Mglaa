Choose a named pattern from the blue selector. The small grid is its threading draft; the larger field combines that draft with its repeating harness sequence. The 48 named patterns use the original threading and a source treadling variation from the two books. Each has its own editable default palette. Each square represents one warp end crossed by one pattern shot; fine tabby binding is omitted so the motif keeps the proportions of the book’s diagrams. The two squares set the pattern and background colors and generate the music together. Click either to open its color grid or exact color picker; the whole textile updates immediately. Their combined hues select the shared harmony. Part One · Part Two, Josephine E. Estes, Miniature Patterns for Hand Weaving.

The left half of the 8 × 4 instrument grid selects the melody sound; the right half selects the pad sound. Each pattern starts with its own melody and pad pairing. Each half keeps one sound selected, and your changes stay with that pattern during the session. Click a square, or use arrow keys while the grid is focused.

Choose Custom to draw on the top draft: click or drag to assign one square per column. The pattern below grows from that sequence. The top draft keeps a fixed width: shorter sequences repeat, and longer sequences stop at the last complete square. The full source pattern still generates the textile.

The two squares are your palette. Click one to open its color grid. The strip underneath changes saturation; the single color square opens an exact color picker.

The combined hues choose a shared chord, with saturated colors having more influence. Low average saturation selects a minor seventh chord; otherwise it selects a major seventh. Every melody note is fitted to this chord.

The two colors alternate musical turns. Each color’s lightness sets its register. Its six hex digits, read as numbers from zero to fifteen, influence note choices and rests. The selected draft’s shaft sequence moves those notes through the shared chord; its harness combinations shape their emphasis. Saturation controls how many turns contain notes.

Playback stays at 84 BPM. Color and draft sequences shape the phrase through the loop. Visual color changes appear immediately; the updated tune starts immediately with a soft transition.

The textile previews the selected structure before playback. During playback, new rows enter at the top; pattern changes start the updated tune immediately. The textile is not a literal piano roll. A filled square is not a separate note. Selecting a new pattern previews its structure throughout the field.

Space starts or pauses. Arrow keys move through the color grid or saturation strip. Escape closes a palette or pauses. Sound pauses when you leave this tab.
